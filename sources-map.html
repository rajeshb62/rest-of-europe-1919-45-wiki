Sources Map

This page maps the wiki’s source pages by what they cover. It will grow as sources are ingested.

Russia / Soviet Union

  • Pokrovsky — Russian History in the Briefest Outline (4th ed., 1933) — Soviet Marxist survey, earliest times to the 1905 revolution; Part III on the revolutionary period 1897–1917 and the defense of October.
  • Short Course of the History of the VKP(b) (1945) — official Stalin-era party history, twelve chapters from 1883 to 1937; the obligatory text of party and civic education.
  • Shestakov (ed.) — Short Course of the History of the USSR (1937) — school textbook (3rd–4th grades) surveying Russian and Soviet history from antiquity to the 1936 Constitution.
  • Iosif Vissarionovich Stalin: A Short Biography (2nd ed., 1948) — official biographical portrait of Stalin, from his birth in Gori (1879) through the Great Patriotic War and post-war reconstruction (1946).
  • Stalin — On the Great Patriotic War of the Soviet Union (1946) — Stalin’s collected wartime speeches, orders and allied correspondence, 3 July 1941 to 3 September 1945; the primary documentary record of the war.

Germany / Weimar Republic & Third Reich

  • Rosenberg — History of the German Republic (1935) — an exile history of the Weimar Republic by the left-socialist historian Arthur Rosenberg, from the 1918 revolution to the death of the republic in 1930.
  • Volz (ed.) — From Great Power to World Power 1937 (1938) — Band 5 of the official Nazi Dokumente der deutschen Politik: 81 German state documents of 1937 — laws, decrees, speeches, notes and protocols — recording the consolidation of the Third Reich and its foreign policy on the eve of the 1938 annexations.
  • Volz (ed.) — Großdeutschland 1938, Band 6 Teil 1 (1940) — Band 6, Part 1 of the official Nazi Dokumente der deutschen Politik: 85 German state documents of 1938 — the Blomberg–Fritsch crisis and Ribbentrop’s appointment as Foreign Minister, the Anschluss of Austria, the Sudeten crisis and Munich Agreement, and the reordering of the enlarged state.
  • Büttner (ed.) — The Road to the National Socialist Reich (1943) — a party-authorized documentary compilation realizing the 25-point program of the NSDAP point by point: the recovery of the Saar, Austria, the Sudetenland, Memel, Danzig and Eupen–Malmedy, the abrogation of Versailles and St. Germain, the Nuremberg racial laws and the exclusion of Jews from the economy, and the labour, agrarian and tax legislation of 1933–1940.
  • Berndt & Kriegk (eds.) — We Experience the Liberation of the Saar (1935) — six German journalists’ eyewitness reports of the Saar campaign and the plebiscite of 13 January 1935, written at the moment of the Saar’s return to the Reich.
  • Friedrichs (comp.) — The National Socialist Revolution 1933, Band 1 (3rd ed., 1938) — the opening volume of the official Nazi Dokumente der deutschen Politik series of the German Academy for Politics: the principal laws, decrees, speeches and documents of the Nazi seizure of power in 1933 — from Hitler’s appointment on 30 January through the Enabling Act and the end of the party state, to the rebuilding of state, economy, culture and law.
  • Friedrichs (comp.) — Der Aufbau des deutschen Führerstaates 1934, Band 2 (3rd ed., 1938) — the second volume of the official Nazi Dokumente der deutschen Politik series and direct companion to Band 1: 92 German state documents of 1934 recording the completion of the Führer-state — the Röhm purge of 30 June, Hitler’s assumption of the head-of-state office on Hindenburg’s death and the plebiscite of 19 August, the German–Polish Non-Aggression Pact, the Saar campaign, and the reordering of state, people, economy, culture and law.
  • Friedrichs (comp.) — Deutschlands Weg zur Freiheit 1935, Band 3 (3rd ed., 1938) — the third volume of the official Nazi Dokumente der deutschen Politik series and direct companion to Bands 1, 2 and 4: 52 German state documents of 1935 — the recovery of the Saar, the reintroduction of general conscription and the building of the Wehrmacht, the Anglo-German Naval Agreement, the Nuremberg racial laws, and the reordering of state, people, economy, culture and law.
  • Friedrichs (comp.) — Deutschlands Aufstieg zur Großmacht 1936, Band 4 (2nd ed., 1938) — the fourth volume of the official Nazi Dokumente der deutschen Politik series and direct continuation of Bands 1, 2 and 3: 64 German state documents of 1936 — the remilitarization of the Rhineland and repudiation of Locarno, the Berlin Olympics, Himmler’s appointment as Chef der deutschen Polizei, the German–Austrian agreement of 11 July, the Anti-Comintern Pact with Japan, and the second Four Year Plan — recording the regime’s consolidation and outward assertion.
  • Geisow — The Soul of the Third Reich (1933) — a völkisch racial-ideological tract written in February 1933, in the weeks after Hitler’s appointment as Reich Chancellor; the unofficial, party-side voice of the Nazi revolution in its first months.
  • Speer — Erinnerungen (1969) — the autobiography of Hitler’s architect and, from 1942, Reich Minister for Armaments and War Production, from his youth and party entry in 1931 through the Third Reich and the war to his capture, the Nuremberg trial and his postwar reflection on responsibility; an insider’s account of Hitler’s circle and the armaments economy.
  • Heiden — Adolf Hitler: Eine Biographie (1936) — the first full-length biography of Hitler, written in exile by the German-Jewish journalist Konrad Heiden and published in Zurich in 1936: from Hitler’s Austrian birth, youth and the “lost years” in Vienna and Munich, through the founding of the NSDAP, the Beer Hall Putsch of 1923 and the writing of Mein Kampf, the years of reconstruction and electoral advance after 1925, to the seizure of power in 1933 and the Night of the Long Knives of 30 June 1934; the critical-democratic German case in “the age of irresponsibility,” counterpart to the regime’s own documentary compilations and to Geisow’s völkisch tract.
  • Hitler — Mein Kampf (Band I 1925, Band II 1926) — the foundational text of National Socialism, dictated in Landsberg prison after the failure of the Beer Hall Putsch and published in two volumes (Eine Abrechnung, 1925; Die nationalsozialistische Bewegung, 1926): the autobiography of Hitler’s Austrian origins, Vienna years and wartime service, and the doctrinal and programmatic core — race and the völkisch state, the Führer principle, propaganda, and the foreign-policy chapters demanding Lebensraum in the East and the destruction of Soviet Russia; the regime’s own foundational text.

Italy / Fascism

  • Businelli — Rivoluzione fascista: Origini e sviluppi del movimento delle camicie nere (1933) — a narrative history of the rise of Italian Fascism written from within the movement by the Fascist educator Alberto Businelli, with a preface by F. T. Marinetti: from the Balkan Wars and the intervention crisis, the founding of the Fasci at Piazza San Sepolcro (1919) and the biennio rosso, to the March on Rome of 1922; the Fascist regime’s own account of its origins and victory.
  • Volpe — Guerra, dopoguerra, fascismo (1928) — the collected wartime-and-Fascist political journalism of the historian Gioacchino Volpe, from 1916 through the Great War, the postwar crisis and the Fascist consolidation of the mid-1920s: morale pieces written for the VIII Armata, essays on France, England, Egypt, regionalism, the school question and Machiavelli, and the explicitly Fascist pieces of 1921–1928; how a professional academic historian of the national school wrote for the interventionist and Fascist press and framed Fascism within Italy’s longer history.
  • Levi — Se questo è un uomo (1947) — the first edition of the most celebrated Italian testimony of the Holocaust: Primo Levi’s account of his deportation from Fossoli to Auschwitz in February 1944 and his survival to the liberation in January 1945 — the arrival and the tattooing of Häftling 174517, the Ka-Be and the selections, the “drowned and the saved,” the chemistry examination, and the closing Storia di dieci giorni; the Italian-Jewish victim’s voice standing at the far end of the fascism the regime sources celebrate.
  • Volpe — History of the Fascist Movement (1936) — the English translation of Volpe’s continuous narrative history of the Fascist movement, published in Rome in the Fascist year XIV (1935–1936): from interventionism and the Fasci d’azione, the founding of the Fasci di combattimento and the movement’s transformation into a party, the March on Rome, Fascism at the helm and the reform of the state, to a closing chapter placing “Fascist Italy” in Europe; the canonical mid-1930s regime historiography of Fascism’s origins by the regime’s foremost academic historian.
  • Farinacci — Storia della Rivoluzione Fascista, Vol. I (Il 1919) (1937) — the first volume of the party history of the Fascist Revolution by the Cremona ras Roberto Farinacci: a polemical insider account of the crisis year 1919 in sixteen chapters and two parts (“Lo sfacelo della classe dirigente,” “La disfatta diplomatica”) — the interventionist split, the founding of the Fasci di combattimento (23 March), the fatti di Milano, the Fiume undertaking (September) and the elections of 16 November.
  • Farinacci — Storia della Rivoluzione Fascista, Vol. II (L’Insurrezione Rossa e la Vittoria dei Fasci) (1938) — the second volume of Farinacci’s party history, covering 1 January 1920 – May 1921: the “red biennium” of strikes, factory occupations and agrarian unrest (L’Insurrezione Rossa) answered by the squadrist reaction and the electoral triumph of the National Blocs (La Vittoria dei Fasci).
  • Farinacci — Storia della Rivoluzione Fascista, Vol. III (La Marcia su Roma) (1939) — the third and final volume of Farinacci’s party history, covering May 1921 – October 1922: from the end of the Giolittian system (Part I, Cap. XXXII–XXXV) through the March on Rome (Part II, Cap. XXXVI–XXXIX) to the formation of the Fascist government.
  • Pini & Bresadola — Storia del Fascismo: Guerra – Rivoluzione – Regime (1928) — a continuous, regime-published chronicle of the rise of Italian Fascism by Giorgio Pini and Federico Bresadola (Libreria del Littorio, Roma 1928): from the fall of Crispi after Adua and the precursor movements, through Nazionalismo e Sindacalismo and the Libyan conquest, interventionism and the Great War, the founding of the Fasci (23 March 1919), Fiume, squadrism and the March on Rome, the Matteotti crisis, the “conquest of the state” (1925–27) and Fascist Italy in the world.
  • Badoglio — Italy in the Second World War: Memories and Documents (1948) — the memoirs of Marshal Pietro Badoglio, the Fascist general who succeeded Mussolini as Prime Minister after the coup of 25 July 1943: Italy in the Second World War from the fall of the regime to the cobelligerence — Mussolini’s April 1939 memorandum to Hitler, the declaration of war of June 1940, the war disasters, the coup d’état of 25 July 1943 and the arrest of Mussolini, the armistice of 8 September 1943, the flight to Brindisi, the cobelligerent government and the liberation of Rome in June 1944; the Allied-side counterpart to the regime’s own histories of Fascism.

France / Third Republic

  • Siegfried — Tableau des partis en France (1930) — a contemporary political geography and sociology of the French party system of the Third Republic, written in 1930 by the founder of French electoral geography: the French character (Part I) and the determining factors of domestic (Part II) and foreign (Part III) policy, the cycles of the postwar Bloc National and Cartel des Gauches (Part IV), the fan of parties from Left to Right (Part V), the closing comparison of Latin and Anglo-Saxon democracy (Part VI), and two statistical appendices on the strength of the parties in the Chamber and the principal votes of 1924–1930; the French case from within the old parliamentary republic of the centre, on the eve of the crises of the 1930s.
  • Halévy — L’ère des tyrannies (1938) — the posthumous essay collection of the historian of English liberalism Élie Halévy, with Célestin Bouglé’s preface: “Sismondi” and the Saint-Simonian economic doctrine, the English policy of social peace, the problem of workers’ control, the present state of the English social question, the interpretation of the world crisis of 1914–1918 (towards revolution, towards war, war and revolution), socialism and democratic parliamentarism, and the closing “L’ère des tyrannies” (1936) — the argument that Fascism, Nazism and Bolshevism are continuations of the revolutionary era transformed by total war and total revolution into the era of the organised, totalitarian state; the French liberal-democratic diagnosis of the totalitarian state, the counterpart to the German, Italian and Russian sources presented from the totalitarian side.
  • Zévaès — Histoire de la Troisième République (1926) — a committed narrative history of the French Third Republic from 1870 to 1926 by the Socialist deputy and historian Alexandre Zévaès: the 4 September 1870, the National Defence and the Commune, the monarchist crisis and the Constitution of 1875, Boulangism and the Panama scandal, the Dreyfus affair, the separation of Church and State, the prewar ministries, the Great War, and the postwar Bloc National and Cartel des Gauches, closing with a Conclusion and annexes tabulating the Presidents, ministries and principal dates of the Republic; the French parliamentary republic told from within by a man of the Left.
  • Daniel Halévy — 1938: Une année d’histoire (1938) — a contemporary month-by-month chronicle of the crisis year 1938 by the historian and man of letters Daniel Halévy, brother of Élie Halévy: the January reopening of the Chamber, his portrait of parliamentary drift and inaction (“la pratique du rien”), a Socialist statesman come to power, the fall of Blum and the ministry of Daladier, the deceptive calm of the summer, the mounting Sudeten crisis and the approach of war, and the closing aftermath of Munich — the French moderate-liberal diary of the year of the Munich Agreement, written at the moment before 1939.
  • Bloch — L’Étrange Défaite (1940) — the celebrated eyewitness account of the collapse of France in 1940 by the historian Marc Bloch, co-founder of the Annales school, written “en pleine rage” in the weeks after the disaster (July–September 1940) and published posthumously in 1946: the presentation of the witness, the deposition of a defeated man from the Intelligence staff of the 1st Army, and the examination of conscience of a Frenchman indicting the military caste, the political class and the whole society that had failed to prepare for total war; the volume appends Bloch’s Testament (18 March 1941) and the clandestine essays written under Vichy (Pourquoi je suis républicain, Sur la réforme de l’enseignement); the French case from the side of the victim and the analyst — the internal diagnosis of the “strange defeat” from a citizen-soldier of the Republic’s academic elite.

Spain / Second Republic & Civil War

  • Madariaga — España: Ensayo de historia contemporánea (1950) — the contemporary history of Spain by the Spanish liberal diplomat and writer Salvador de Madariaga, 1950 Buenos Aires fifth edition (first published in English as Spain 1930, in Spanish 1931): Libro Primero, from the land, people and empire (Chs. I–VIII), the elements of Alfonso XIII’s reign — the agrarian, labour, church, army, Catalan (three chapters), Basque and Galician, Portuguese, Moroccan, Hispanoamerican and foreign questions (Chs. IX–XXI) — and the reign of Alfonso XIII itself, the prewar, the Great War, postwar liberal crisis, the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera and its liquidation, to the situation of 1930 (Chs. XXII–XXVII); Libro Segundo, the Republic of 1931 through its left and right phases and the Popular Front (Chs. I–VII), the Civil War — the battle of the three Franciscos, the national phase and the phases of foreign infiltration and domination, the role of the Spanish Communist Party and Russian influence, and the war’s end (Chs. VIII–XI) — and Franco’s early dictatorship, its interior and foreign policy and the éxodo (Chs. XII–XV), closing with five appendices (Memoria personal; the Stalin letter to Largo Caballero; the Companys letter to Negrín; and two articles on Azaña and on civil war from Ahora, March 1935) and the name and general indexes; the Spanish liberal-democratic case from within — the victim-analyst of the Civil War, in the manner of Bloch on the French defeat, the peninsular counterpart to the regime sources and the French and Italian pages.
  • Chaves Nogales — A sangre y fuego: Héroes, bestias y mártires de España (1937) — nine stories of the Spanish Civil War written in exile in Paris in 1937 by the Sevillian liberal journalist Manuel Chaves Nogales (first published 1937): the author’s prologue (Montrouge, January–May 1937) and the nine novelas — ¡Masacre, masacre!, La gesta de los caballistas, Y a lo lejos, una lucecita, La columna de hierro, El tesoro de Briesca, Los guerreros marroquíes, ¡Viva la muerte!, Bigornia and Consejo obrero — each drawn, per the author’s NOTA, from a rigorously veridical fact; the literary testimony of the war itself from the liberal “third Spain” that refused solidarity with the assassins of either side.
  • Fernández Almagro — Historia del reinado de Alfonso XIII (4th ed., 1977) — the Spanish monarchy-centered institutional chronicle of the whole reign of Alfonso XIII, 1902–1931, by the Granada historian and critic Melchor Fernández Almagro (4th ed., Montaner y Simón, Barcelona, 1977): a strictly political and external history in six Parts — I. Liquidación de la Regencia (1902–1906), II. Intentos de renovación (1907–1912), III. Equilibrio inestable (1912–1917), IV. Conatos revolucionarios (1917–1923), V. La Dictadura (1923–1930) and VI. Capitulación de la Monarquía (1930–1931) — each government and each royal intervention from Sagasta through the Moroccan wars, the Juntas de Defensa and the general strike of 1917, the pistolerismo of Barcelona, the disaster of Annual, the Dictatorship of Primo de Rivera, and the monarchy’s fall in the municipal elections of 12 April 1931; the antecedent to the republic and Civil War covered by Madariaga and Chaves Nogales.
  • Azaña — Mi rebelión en Barcelona (1935) — the prison memoir of the October 1934 events written by the liberal republican statesman Manuel Azaña (former prime minister, 1931–1933) while held, first aboard the Uruguay and then aboard the prison ships Ciudad de Cádiz and Barcaiztegui, 2nd edition 1935, dedicated to Ángel Ossorio: the title answering his own indictment for rebellion, and six sections of narrative (Barcelona in November aboard the Galiano, through December aboard the Barcaiztegui) giving his account of the general strike, the proclamation of the Catalan State by the Generalidad and its crushing under martial law, and his own arrest and imprisonment; the volume adds his parliamentary speech of 25 June 1934 on the Catalan law of land-cultivation contracts, his address to the Catalan republicans of 10 August 1934, and a legal appendix reproducing the papers of his prosecution — the cipher telegram of 10 October 1934 recording his detention, the fiscal’s dictamen, the statement of HECHOS (the proclamation of the Estado Catalán on the night of 6 October and the state of war), and the numbered juridical studies (El estado de guerra, La garantía de las setenta y dos horas, Un delito patente) — closing with the Supreme Court’s sobreseimiento libre; the accused statesman’s own voice at the moment the Republic’s internal crisis broke in Catalonia.
  • 1940) — the Nationalist side’s own authorized military history of the Spanish Civil War by the war correspondent and propagandist Manuel Aznar Zubigaray (1940; dedicated to his three sons “combatientes de España”): thirty-four chapters across three volumes tracing the war as a sequence of campaigns and maneuvers — the alzamiento of 18 July 1936, the march on Madrid and the failed assault, the isolated strongholds (Alcázar, Oviedo, Santa María de la Cabeza), Málaga, the Jarama, Guadalajara, the northern front and the conquest of Bilbao, Brunete, Santander, Asturias, Teruel, the great battle of the Ebro, the Battle of Catalonia and the fall of Barcelona, and the final offensive of 1939 — closing with a summary of the reasons for Franco’s victory; the regime’s military-strategic voice on the 1936–39 war, complementary to Madariaga’s liberal history and to Chaves Nogales’s testimony.
  • Hayes — Misión de Guerra en España, 1942–1945 (1945) — the memoir of Carlton J. H. Hayes, the Columbia historian who served as U.S. Ambassador to Franco’s Spain from May 1942 to January 1945 (Spanish translation of Wartime Mission in Spain, 1942–1945, Macmillan 1945): nine chapters on his double mission to counter Axis pressure on Spain and keep it out of the war while winning Allied facilities — his appointment and first contacts, the struggle with Spanish non-belligerency, the winning of facilities, the opposing currents of Axis agents, the progress of Spanish neutrality, the wolfram crisis, Spain’s growing benevolent neutrality, and his departure in January 1945; the American ambassador’s Allied-side eyewitness account of Spain’s wartime neutrality, covering the war years 1942–45 beside Tusell’s chapters on the same period.
  • Tusell — Historia de España en el siglo XX, II. La crisis de los años treinta: República y guerra civil (2000) — continuous scholarly history of the Second Republic and the Civil War by Javier Tusell (UNED), volume II of his four-volume Historia de España en el siglo XX (2000; ePubLibre edition): society and economy at the height of 1930 (the agrarian and urban world, everyday life, the impact of the world crisis, the coming of mass democracy), the Republic from the 1931 experience through the Constitution, the reformist biennium (military, religious, agrarian, social and economic reforms), the «República en una tenaza», the conservative biennium of radicals and cedistas, the October 1934 insurrection and its aftermath, the split of the socialists, Spanish fascism, the Popular Front of 1936 and the “plano inclinado” toward war, and then the Civil War from conspiracy and the balance of forces through revolution, repression, the church, the column-war, Madrid, internationalisation, the northern campaign, the two armies, Franco’s political unity, the Popular Front’s internal confrontation, Negrín, Teruel, the Ebro and the war’s end, closing with society and culture in wartime and a long bibliography; the Republic-and-Civil-War companion to the wiki’s Tusell volume III, the modern Spanish scholarly backbone for the crisis years the wiki’s contemporary Spanish sources (Aznar, Chaves Nogales, Hayes, Azaña, Madariaga) enter from within. Volume IV (La transición democrática) belongs with Rest of Europe 1946–present when processed.
  • Tusell — Historia de España en el siglo XX, III. La dictadura de Franco (2000) — continuous scholarly history of Franco’s dictatorship by Javier Tusell (UNED), volume III of his four-volume Historia de España en el siglo XX (2000; ePubLibre edition): from the last war bulletin of 1 April 1939 to Franco’s death in November 1975 — the dictator, the regime as dictatorship, repression and exile, fascist temptation and wartime foreign policy 1939–45, isolation and autarky, Concordat and US pacts of 1953, developmentalism and opposition 1951–65, tardofranquismo of Carrero and Arias Navarro; the modern Spanish analytical backbone for the Franco years the wiki’s contemporary Spanish sources enter only in part. Volume IV (La transición democrática) belongs with Rest of Europe 1946–present when processed.

World War II / Espionage

  • Singer — Spies and Traitors of World War II (1945) — a wartime journalistic survey of espionage in the Second World War by the American writer Kurt Singer, published by Prentice-Hall in 1945: twenty-six spy stories and network profiles ranging across the secret services of both sides — Admiral Wilhelm Canaris and the German Abwehr, Lavrenti Beria and the Soviet secret service at Lubianka Street, the Japanese networks, the saboteurs landed in the United States, the Nazi weather-station espionage in the Arctic, German and Japanese illicit radio, the Nazi spy web in Denmark, agents in neutral Ireland, Baltic espionage, Canaris’s hand in the Arab world and his agents hunting Maurice Mercier through Spain and Portugal, the De Gaulle agents in Africa, and the secret plan for a postwar Nazi underground in Argentina; written partly from the author’s own work exposing Axis agents since 1933, with names of Allied agents still at work concealed and some incidents reconstructed — the Allied-side, secondary account of the secret war that the regime and memoir sources in this wiki describe from within.
