Deutschlands Aufstieg zur Großmacht 1936 (Band 4, 2nd ed. 1938)

Identity

Deutschlands Aufstieg zur Großmacht 1936 (Germany’s Rise to Great Power, 1936) is Band 4 of Dokumente der deutschen Politik — the official documentary series of the Deutsche Hochschule für Politik (German Academy for Politics). It was compiled by Axel Friedrichs, under the general editorship of Paul Meier-Benneckenstein (Regierungsrat, President of the Hochschule für Politik), and published by Junker und Dünnhaupt Verlag, Berlin. Title-page copyright 1937; this is the 2nd edition, 1938. The volume collects the principal laws, decrees, speeches, notes and public documents of the year 1936 — the year of the Rhineland remilitarization, the Berlin Olympics, the Four Year Plan and the Anti-Comintern Pact.

It is the direct continuation of Friedrichs’ Band 1 (Die nationalsozialistische Revolution 1933), Band 2 (Der Aufbau des deutschen Führerstaates 1934) and Band 3 (Deutschlands Weg zur Freiheit 1935, all in this wiki): it covers the year between Band 3 and Volz’s 1937 volume (Band 5), and is the Dokumente der deutschen Politik volume in the wiki covering the pivotal year 1936, when the regime combined the staging of the Olympic Games with the remilitarization of the Rhineland and the launching of the Four Year Plan.

Character of the source

A government-sponsored source book, identical in character to Bands 1 and 2: an internal, regime-sanctioned compilation of authoritative state documents — laws, decrees, party orders, Hitler’s and other leaders’ speeches, and diplomatic notes — with framing commentary from the editor. The year 1936 was the regime’s annus mirabilis of consolidation and external assertiveness: the remilitarization of the Rhineland (7 March) and the repudiation of Locarno, the overwhelming Reichstag election of 29 March, the Berlin Olympics, the appointment of Heinrich Himmler as Chef der deutschen Polizei (17 June), the German–Austrian agreement of 11 July, the recognition of Italian Abyssinia and of Franco’s government, the Anti-Comintern Pact with Japan (25 November), and the proclamation of the second Four Year Plan (18 October). It complements the other regime-published documentary volumes in the Germany group.

Contents (structure)

The volume is organized into three parts, with 64 numbered documents:

  • A. Der nationalsozialistische Staat und die nationalsozialistische Bewegung — the National Socialist state and movement (documents 1–6): Goebbels’ speech at the Berlin Gau-tag on the work of the state and party (17 January); Lutze’s speech before the Diplomatic Corps on the nature and tasks of the SA (24 January); the Führer’s decree appointing a Chef der deutschen Polizei and naming Heinrich Himmler to the office (17 June); the Führer’s proclamation opening the 8th Party Congress, the “Parteitag der Ehre,” at Nuremberg (9 September); Goebbels’ and Rosenberg’s party-congress speeches on the world danger of Bolshevism and the decisive world struggle (10 September)
  • B. Der Kampf um den Frieden und die deutsche Ehre — the struggle for peace and German honour (documents 7–32): Hitler’s interview with Bertrand de Jouvenel on Franco-German relations (21 February); Hitler’s Reichstag speech on the restoration of German sovereignty over the Rhineland and his plan for European peace (7 March); the Reich government’s memorandum to the Locarno signatory powers and the decree dissolving the Reichstag (7 March); Blomberg’s Heroes’ Memorial Day speech (8 March); the official statement on the German step and peace plan (12 March); Ribbentrop’s speech and protest before the League of Nations Council in London on the Soviet pact, Locarno and the German peace plan (19 March); the result of the Reichstag election (29 March); the Reich government’s peace plan (31 March); the German front-fighters’ answer to the French (16 May); the German–Austrian agreement of 11 July; the note to François-Poncet on non-intervention in the Spanish Civil War (17 August); the decree on the two-year term of service (24 August) and the NSK communiqué justifying it as self-defence against Soviet imperialism; the notes on the Spanish Civil War, Abyssinia and the German rivers; the recognition of the Franco government (18 November); Ribbentrop’s note to Vansittart on Germany’s accession to the U-boat-warfare agreement (23 November); the Anti-Comintern Pact with Japan (25 November) with the declarations of Goebbels and Ribbentrop; the answer to the Anglo-French mediation proposals on Spain (12 December); and Ribbentrop’s Anglo-German Fellowship speech on colonial equality (15 December)
  • C. Die Neuordnung von Staat, Volk, Wirtschaft, Kultur und Recht — the reordering of state, people, economy, culture and law (documents 33–64), in five thematic sections:
    1. Staatsordnung, Verfassung und Verwaltung (33–36): Heß’ speech before the civil service on officialdom in the National Socialist state (4 June); Frick’s speeches on the rebuilding of communal administration (14 September) and “Neubau des Reiches” at the Ordensburg Vogelsang (19 November); the law on the constitution and administration of the Reich capital Berlin (1 December)
    2. Bevölkerungs- und Rassenpolitik (37–38): the law amending the Defence Law on the active military service of Jews (26 June); Frank’s speech on Judaism in legal science (3 October)
    3. Neubau der wirtschaftlichen und sozialen Ordnung (39–51), subdivided into a) the struggle for the worker and the new social constitution (Goebbels’ call for the National Day of the German People, 25 April; the decree on the Reich Labour Service, 26 September; the Winterhilfswerk law, 1 December) and b) the rebuilding of the economy and the Four Year Plan (Schwerin von Krosigk’s speech on financial policy, 6 February; Schacht’s declaration on currency policy, 30 September; Heß’ Hof speech on Germany’s struggle for economic freedom, 11 October; the Führer’s decree implementing the Four Year Plan, 18 October; Göring’s Sportpalast speech on the second Four Year Plan, 28 October; the law appointing a Reich Commissioner for Price Formation, 29 October; the law against economic sabotage, 1 December; Reinhardt’s speech on tax reform, 3 December; Schacht’s Frankfurt lecture, 9 December; and Schacht’s article “Deutschlands Kolonialproblem” in Foreign Affairs, December)
    4. Neubau der Kultur (52–57): Rust’s Heidelberg speech on “Nationalsozialismus und Wissenschaft” at the university’s 550th anniversary (29 June); Goebbels’ decree on the reform of art criticism (26 November) and his speech to the Reichskulturkammer (27 November); the Hitler-Jugend law (1 December) with Schirach’s declaration and press speech (2 and 7 December)
    5. Neugestaltung des Rechts (58–64): Frank’s theses on the position of the judge (14 January) and his speech on the foundations of the National Socialist conception of law (29 January); the law on the People’s Court (18 April); Frank’s speech opening the German Jurists’ Day at Leipzig (16 May); the law against kidnapping for ransom (22 June); the law re-establishing a Supreme Court of the Wehrmacht (26 June); and Gürtner’s essay on “Die Erneuerung des deutschen Strafrechtes” (5 November)

Back matter: Zeittafel und Chronologie der wiedergegebenen Dokumente (chronological table, p. 358) and Sach- und Namen-Gesamtregister (general name and subject index, p. 367), the latter a series-wide register referencing all four volumes (I–IV) of the Dokumente der deutschen Politik.

OCR / extraction quality

Source is a University of Florida Library scan (Smalthers Libraries; shelfmark DD253 .A6 Bd. 4, 1938) delivered as a 404-file paginated text dump (Anna’s Archive). The book is set in Fraktur type; the embedded text layer is a Fraktur OCR of good but not perfect quality — clean and fully readable, though it shows the characteristic Fraktur-model misreadings (e.g. “Luke” for Lutze, “Gesek/Gesez” for Gesetz, “deursche” for deutsche, “Aufsak/Aufsah/Aussak” for Aufsatz). Per protocol this is the extraction source (the dump carries no image pages, so no re-OCR pass was possible). Result: clean, readable German transcription assembled from the 404 ordered page files (18,216 lines; 403 page-form-feeds; no page gaps — the printed text closes with the series register at physical p. 380, followed only by blank leaves and the library date-due card). The structure above has been reconstructed and cross-checked against the printed Inhaltsverzeichnis and body headings.
