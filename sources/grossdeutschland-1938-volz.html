Großdeutschland 1938

Hans Volz (ed.) — Großdeutschland 1938 (Greater Germany 1938), Band 6 / Teil 1 of Dokumente der deutschen Politik (Documents of German Politics), founded by the Hochschule für Politik (Academy for Politics) and continued by the Deutsches Auslandswissenschaftliches Institut (German Institute for Foreign Affairs), edited by Prof. Dr. F. A. Six. Junker und Dünnhaupt Verlag, Berlin, 2nd edition 1940 (first edition 1939). An official Nazi compilation of 85 German state documents of the year 1938 — laws, decrees, speeches, notes and protocols — recording the most decisive year of the Third Reich: the Blomberg–Fritsch crisis and the reconstruction of the Wehrmacht command, the annexation of Austria, the Sudeten crisis and the Munich Agreement, and the reordering of the enlarged state.

EditorDr. Hans Volz (1904–1978), head of the library of the Hochschule für Politik, Berlin; series continued under Prof. Dr. F. A. Six, director of the Deutsches Auslandswissenschaftliches Institut
SeriesDokumente der deutschen Politik, Band 6 (following Band 5, Von der Großmacht zur Weltmacht 1937, also in this wiki; the earlier volumes Band 1, Die nationalsozialistische Revolution 1933, and Band 2, Der Aufbau des deutschen Führerstaates 1934, are likewise held); Band 6 is issued in two parts, of which this scan is Teil 1
Edition2. Auflage 1940 (first edition 1939), Junker und Dünnhaupt Verlag, Berlin; copyright 1940, printed in Germany by Clemens Landgraf Nachf. W. Stolle, Dresden-Freital; the University of Florida Libraries copy (OCLC 6853113)
Structure85 numbered documents in three main parts, with a Zeittafel und Chronologie (chronological table), Personenregister and Sachregister (name and subject indexes); 18,884 lines of extracted text
Period1938: documents dated January–December 1938, covering the sixth year of the National Socialist regime and the territorial expansion that made the Third Reich “Greater Germany”
NoteThe filename of the source file reads “Belarus, 1940” — this is erroneous archive metadata; the title page confirms Junker und Dünnhaupt Verlag, Berlin, 2. Auflage 1940. A companion to the other Dokumente der deutschen Politik volumes in this wiki — the Friedrichs-compiled Bände 1, 2, 3 and 4 (1933, 1934, 1935 and 1936) and Volz’s Band 5 for 1937 — reproducing the original German text of laws, decrees and official communications verbatim

Contents. The volume documents the decisive year 1938 of the Third Reich in three parts. Part A (documents 1–20) records the National Socialist state and movement: the creation of the Secret Cabinet Council and the dismissal of Neurath and appointment of Ribbentrop as Foreign Minister (4 February 1938) at the height of the Blomberg–Fritsch crisis; Hitler’s Reichstag speech of 20 February 1938; the decree on a Reich Chancellery office for the Sudeten question; Goebbels’ speech “Democracy and Bolshevism” at the Nuremberg party congress (10 September 1938); Hitler’s speeches at Saarbrücken, Weimar and the Munich Bürgerbräukeller (October–November 1938); and the decree instituting the Cross of Honour of the German Mother (16 December 1938). Part B records foreign policy: Germany’s relations with Italy, Hungary, Japan and Manchukuo. Part C records the establishment of Greater Germany — the Anschluss of Austria (the German march into Austria of 12 March 1938, the law of reunion of 13 March 1938, the plebiscite of 10 April 1938) and the annexation of the Sudetenland (the Munich Agreement of 29 September 1938 and the law on the reunion of the Sudeten territories of 21 November 1938), closing with a reprinted Czech account of the Munich cession.

Character of the source. An official documentary publication of the National Socialist state, reproducing the original texts of German laws, decrees, speeches, notes and protocols of the year 1938 in the German original, published in Berlin by the party-supporting Junker und Dünnhaupt house under the auspices of the Deutsches Auslandswissenschaftliches Institut (directed by F. A. Six, an SS-Brigadeführer). It is a primary source in the strictest sense — the laws themselves (the law of reunion with Austria, the law on the reunification of the Sudeten territories), the verbatim official communications of the Reich government, and the speeches of Hitler, Goebbels, Frick and Göring as officially released. Edited from within the regime to document the state’s own account of its expansion, it is at once a record of fact and an instrument of propaganda; it supplies the official internal voice of the German dictatorship in the year of its greatest territorial triumph, standing opposite the exile histories (such as Rosenberg’s History of the German Republic, also in this wiki).

Quality. Good. Extracted from a 432-file paginated text dump (University of Florida scan) by concatenating the numbered page files in order. Text is clean and continuous; laws, decrees, official notes and speech texts are preserved verbatim with footnotes. Section and document headings and dates verified against the printed Inhaltsverzeichnis (table of contents). A. Der nationalsozialistische Staat und die nationalsozialistische Bewegung (The National Socialist State and the National Socialist Movement)

Dokumente 1–20

The consolidation of the regime in the year of expansion. The decree establishing a Secret Cabinet Council and the Führer’s letter of thanks to Freiherr von Neurath (4 February 1938); the official announcement of Ribbentrop’s appointment as Reich Foreign Minister and the recall of the German ambassadors in London, Rome, Tokyo and Vienna (4 February 1938); Hitler’s Reichstag speech in the Kroll Opera House (20 February 1938); the amnesty for party jurisdiction (27 April 1938); Hitler’s speeches at the Berlin Olympiastadion and Lustgarten on 1 May 1938; the Blood Order award (30 May 1938); Hitler’s proclamation to the “Party Congress of Greater Germany” at Nuremberg (6 September 1938); Goebbels’ speech “Democracy and Bolshevism” (10 September 1938); Hitler’s speech at Saarbrücken (9 October 1938); his speeches at Weimar and to his old comrades in the Munich Bürgerbräukeller (6 and 8 November 1938); and the decree instituting the Cross of Honour of the German Mother (16 December 1938).

B. Deutschlands Außenpolitik (Germany’s Foreign Policy)

I. Deutschlands Verhältnis zu Italien, Ungarn, Japan und Mandschukuo (Germany’s Relations with Italy, Hungary, Japan and Manchukuo)

Germany’s relations with the Axis and its partners as officially documented in 1938: the treaties and communiqués with Italy (the Rome–Berlin Axis), the recognition of Manchukuo, and the strengthening of relations with Hungary and Japan in the year of the Anti-Comintern front.

C. Die Errichtung des Großdeutschen Reiches (The Establishment of Greater Germany)

I. Der Anschluß Österreichs (The Anschluss of Austria)

The German annexation of Austria as officially documented: the entry of German troops on 12 March 1938; the law of 13 March 1938 on the reunion of Austria with the German Reich; the Reich governor arrangements; the ordinance introducing the Nuremberg race laws into Austria (20 May 1938); the plebiscite of 10 April 1938 and its result; the administrative incorporation of the “Ostmark” into the Reich.

II. Der Anschluß des Sudetengebietes (The Annexation of the Sudetenland)

The Sudeten crisis and its resolution as officially documented: the German-Czechoslovak declaration on the protection of the mutual Volksgruppen (20 November 1938); the final delimitation of the German-Czechoslovak frontier (21 November 1938); the law on the reunion of the Sudeten German territories with the German Reich (21 November 1938); Hitler’s speech at the Sudeten supplementary elections in Reichenberg (2 December 1938) and their result (4 December 1938); the ordinances introducing Reich Labour Service and the Nuremberg race laws into the Sudeten territories (6 and 27 December 1938). The volume closes with the reprinted text of the Munich Agreement of 29 September 1938 and a Czech account of the cession of the Sudetenland.

Zeittafel und Chronologie (Chronological Table), Registers

The volume closes with a Zeittafel und Chronologie of the reproduced documents month by month, together with Personen- and Sachregister (name and subject indexes) and maps drawn by Alois Moser (pp. 224 and 368) of the territorial changes of 1938.
