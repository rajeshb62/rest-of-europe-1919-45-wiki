Die Seele des Dritten Reiches

Hans Geisow — Die Seele des Dritten Reiches (The Soul of the Third Reich), 2nd edition, Armanen-Verlag, Leipzig, 1933 (title page dated 1933; preface signed Frankfurt a. M., February 1933). A völkisch tract on the racial and spiritual foundations of the National Socialist revolution, written in the weeks immediately after Hitler’s appointment as Reich Chancellor on 30 January 1933 and before the seizure of absolute power later that year. 93 pages.

AuthorHans Geisow (the name is OCR’d “Hans Beifow” on the scan); a völkisch author of the Armanen circle
PublisherArmanen-Verlag, Leipzig (Hofpitalstraße 10), the occultist-völkisch house founded by Guido von List’s successor and associated with the “Armanen” racial-mystical school; 2nd edition (Zweite Auflage)
Edition1933, Armanen-Verlag, Leipzig; printed by Pierer’sche Hofbuchdruckerei Stephan Geibel & Co., Altenburg, Thür.; title page carried the note “Zweite Auflage”
StructureA preface (Vorwort) and fourteen chapters of continuous völkisch argument on race, soul, history, liberalism, economics, family, church, art and education; 2,742 lines of extracted text
PeriodThe weeks around February 1933 — a contemporary record of the mood of German völkisch nationalism at the moment of Hitler’s appointment, treating the revolution as “a piece of religion”
NotePublisher’s advertisement for the Armanen “Neue Bücher zur Staats- und Kulturpolitik” series appears on the final page of the scan and is not part of the text

Contents. The book is an essay in racial-mystical politics, arguing that German history and German decline must be understood through race and “the soul of the people.” The preface opens with Hitler’s appointment of 30 January 1933 and the promise of a Volksgemeinschaft (national community) overcoming class divisions. The opening chapter, “Weltenwende” (Turn of the Age), holds that history is a piece of nature and that the forces of race and blood are the true currents beneath political events. The chapters then move through the crisis of the present age (“Unsere Zeit und ihre Krankheit”), the racial idea (“Der Rassegedanke”), the origins of mankind (“Vom Ursprung der Menschheit” — drawing on the researches of Hermann Wirth), the creativity of the Nordic man (“Das Schöpfertum des nordischen Menschen”), race-specific thought (“Das arteigene Denken”), the psychic current in German history (“Der seelische Kraftstrom in der deutschen Geschichte”), and a long polemic against liberalism (“Der Liberalismus”) as an alien, Jewish-masonic mode of thought born of the French Revolution. The second half applies the argument to economic life (“Das Wirtschaftsleben”), the family (“Die Familie”), the church (“Die Kirche”), art, and education (“Die Erziehung”), ending with the conviction that God, not man, makes world history and that the German’s struggle for his right to life is a piece of religion.

Character of the source. A primary document of German völkisch-nationalist ideology at the moment of the National Socialist seizure of power, written by a party-adherent author and published by the Armanen occultist-völkisch house in the spring of 1933. It belongs to the same family of racial-theoretical texts that supplied the ideological accompaniment to the Nazi revolution, and it shows the theological-racial vocabulary — blood, soul, Nordic race, the enemy Jew and freemasonry, the rejection of liberalism and individualism — in which that revolution presented itself to its German public in its first months. As a window on the mental world of the movement’s own adherents in 1933 it complements the official document collections of the same period (such as Volz’s Von der Großmacht zur Weltmacht, also in this wiki), being the unofficial, party-side voice rather than the state record.

Vorwort (Preface)

Signed Frankfurt a. M., February 1933. Opens with the appointment of Adolf Hitler as Reich Chancellor on 30 January 1933 and the advent of a movement whose goal is a Volksgemeinschaft overcoming class antagonisms.

I. Weltenwende (Turn of the Age)

History is a piece of nature; the path of nature is never straight. Race and blood, not political arrangement, are the deep currents beneath events. God makes world history through the nature and blood He gives to men and peoples.

II. Unsere Zeit und ihre Krankheit (Our Time and its Sickness)

The present age is diseased; the roots of the German people’s health lie in its own inherited being. The task of the time is to learn the soil and life-sap from which the people draws its strength.

III. Der Rassegedanke (The Racial Idea)

The racial idea as the governing principle of German renewal. (Heading misread by OCR; chapter content identified by position.)

IV. Vom Ursprung der Menschheit (On the Origin of Mankind)

Draws on the researches of Hermann Wirth to extend the history of mankind some fifteen to twenty thousand years back, and reads the Bible — including the figure of Jesus — in the light of Nordic racial prehistory.

V. Das Schöpfertum des nordischen Menschen (The Creativity of the Nordic Man)

Creative spirit as a chief psychic mark of the Nordic man; the creative capacity of the race as the source of German culture.

VI. Das arteigene Denken (Race-Specific Thought)

Thought is race-bound; the German must think out of his own inherited being rather than alien categories.

VII. Der seelische Kraftstrom in der deutschen Geschichte (The Psychic Current in German History)

The hidden spiritual current that carried German history — from the struggles of the Reformation (Luther, Münzer) to the call to become a people. (Chapter numeral misread by OCR.)

VIII. Der Liberalismus (Liberalism)

The central polemic: liberalism as an alien, artfremd way of viewing life, an instrument of Jewish-masonic forces behind the French Revolution; the “freedom, equality, fraternity” of the Revolution as deception; the organic people as against the dismembering, individualist liberalism.

IX. (On the arts under liberalism)

The dissolving effect of liberalism on art, the university and culture; the loss of the people’s rootedness in its homeland; against “world literature” as against great creative individual spirits. (Heading misread; content recovered.)

X. Das Wirtschaftsleben (Economic Life)

The economy must be restored to its bond with the people; against liberal economics, world economy and the argument for autarky in a seventy-million nation; the economy exists for life, not life for the economy.

XI. Die Familie (The Family)

The family as the innermost cell of a people’s life; the state’s task in strengthening it; the man’s duty as father and example in the circle of wife and children.

XII. Die Kirche (The Church)

The churches and the racial renewal: the brown fighters for Hitler’s German renewal are forbidden to enter into disputes of belief; the Catholic church knows no democracy; Luther’s work not yet complete; the need for the church to grasp the blood-bound, God-given order of the people.

XIII. (Art)

Architecture, poetry, music and the other arts in the service of the people; the artist’s great responsibility; art must find again the soil from which it springs. (Heading misread; content recovered.)

XIV. Die Erziehung (Education)

The renewal of life is above all a work of education of the German people; the reconciliation of age and youth; the excesses of revolutionary movements pass like a pendulum; God, not man, makes world history — to be German is to be a whole man, and the fight for German Lebensrecht is a piece of religion.
