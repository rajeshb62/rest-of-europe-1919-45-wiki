Manuel Aznar Zubigaray — Historia militar de la Guerra de España, Tomos I–III

Full bibliographic citation — Manuel Aznar Zubigaray (1894–1975). Historia militar de la Guerra de España. — Tomo I (1940), Tomo II (1940), Tomo III (1939/1940). This text from the ePubLibre digital editions (ePub r1.0, “Titivillus,” 2018). Dedication: “A mis hijos Manuel, Javier y José María, combatientes de España.”

Manuel Aznar Zubigaray, the Navarrese journalist who became the Nationalist side’s principal war correspondent and propagandist (and grandfather of the future prime minister José María Aznar), wrote this as the authorized military history of the Nationalist war effort. His NOTA PREVIA explains the work’s aim — to place before the general reader “un relato sistemático… de la guerra de liberación de España,” to show that an army is “un sistema nacional en marcha,” and to “vulgarize the thoughts and plans that were born in the mind of the Generalísimo Franco to attain victory.” It covers only the operations of the Army of the land; the maritime and air war and the interior politics are touched upon only in passing. It is the Spanish national case — the military chronicle of the war from the side that won it, written while the fighting had barely ended, the direct counterpart to Madariaga’s liberal political history and to the regime sources elsewhere in the wiki.

The three volumes run ~37,000 lines combined (Tomo I: 13,883; Tomo II: 11,977; Tomo III: 12,194), extracted from EPUB containers, clean text layer, no OCR required.

Content Summary — Tomo I (1936–1937)

Opening with the author’s manifesto of purpose, the volume covers the outbreak and first year of the war:

  • Cap. Primero — Acción y reacción del Ejército nacional. The Movimiento of 18 July 1936 as military and popular, argued (citing Ortega y Gasset’s “Epílogo para ingleses”) not to be a mere pronunciamiento.
  • Cap. II — El alzamiento del Ejército y de los partidos nacionales. Popular support for the army; the mobilization of Falange Española.
  • Cap. III — Situación estratégica general; problema de las comunicaciones. Strategy of the war, and the communications problem on land, sea and air.
  • Cap. IV — Maniobras sobre las fronteras de Portugal y Francia; marcha hacia Madrid. Operations along the two frontiers and the early march on Madrid.
  • Cap. V — Sobre la frontera francesa. The frustrated relief of the San Sebastián garrison and combats in the north.
  • Cap. VI — La guerra en los frentes llamados secundarios: Aragón, Baleares, Andalucía, Santander y Asturias. The “secondary” fronts.
  • Cap. VII — Comienza la maniobra para el cerco y asalto de Madrid. The opening of the encirclement and assault on Madrid.
  • Cap. VIII — Tres centros aislados de resistencia: el Alcázar de Toledo, Oviedo, Santa María de la Cabeza. The three isolated strongholds.
  • Cap. IX — Conversión del frente nacional para la liberación de Toledo y maniobra de aproximación a Madrid.
  • Cap. X — El ataque frustrado sobre Madrid; un arpón clavado en los suburbios; la guerra cambia de signo. The failed assault on Madrid and the turning of the war.
  • Cap. XI — Final del año 1936; Franco Generalísimo de los Ejércitos y Jefe del Estado; notas sobre la situación general.

Content Summary — Tomo II (1937–1938)

  • Cap. Primero — Año 1937. La batalla del cerco de Madrid. The battles for the encirclement of Madrid.
  • Cap. II — La batalla de Málaga. The fall of Málaga (February 1937); the siege operations shifting to the right wing; the Red reaction in Asturias.
  • Cap. III — Continúan las operaciones del cerco de Madrid. The great battles on the banks of the Jarama and the cutting of the road.
  • Cap. IV — Operaciones de Guadalajara y sus consecuencias militares y políticas. The Guadalajara battle (March 1937) and its consequences.
  • Cap. V — La campaña del Norte; conquista de Bilbao. The northern campaign and the conquest of Bilbao (June 1937).
  • Cap. VI — Batalla de Brunete. The Red army’s first great strategic diversion (July 1937).
  • Cap. VII — La maniobra sobre Santander. The advance on Santander (August 1937).
  • Cap. VIII — Belchite; el frente de Aragón durante el año 1937. The second Red strategic diversion against Zaragoza (September 1937).
  • Cap. IX — Reconquista de Asturias por Franco; «El frente norte ha desaparecido». The reconquest of Asturias and the end of the northern front (October 1937).
  • Cap. X — Pausa, planes en Aragón, preparación de una nueva ofensiva nacional en Guadalajara y gran ataque general del Ejército rojo en Teruel. The pause and the approach of the Teruel battle.
  • Cap. XI — Año 1938. Gran batalla de Teruel y del Alfambra. The great battle of Teruel (December 1937–February 1938) fought in the snow.
  • Cap. XII — Martirio y gloria de la ciudad de Huesca. The siege and defense of Huesca.

Content Summary — Tomo III (1938–1939)

  • Cap. Primero — La batalla de Aragón o «Gran batalla de Franco». The Aragon offensive; the Generalísimo’s strategy widening its scope (spring 1938).
  • Cap. II — Desarrollo y triunfo de la batalla de Franco o de Aragón; cinco Cuerpos de Ejército y cinco marchas.
  • Cap. III — Continúa el relato de la batalla de Aragón; segundo tiempo de la ofensiva. Operations north and south of the Ebro, toward Catalonia and the Mediterranean.
  • Cap. IV — Campaña de Teruel y de Castellón de la Plana; reacción roja en el Segre.
  • Cap. V — Batalla hacia Sagunto y Segorbe. The National operation in Extremadura; the Red strategic diversion on the Ebro.
  • Cap. VI — Gran batalla del Ebro. The great battle of the Ebro (July–November 1938), including the Red plan per the Orden general de operaciones número 2.
  • Cap. VII — Gran batalla del Ebro (fases tercera y cuarta; desgaste y contraofensiva).
  • Cap. VIII — Episodios en el río Segre y en Extremadura; intermedio de dos batallas.
  • Cap. IX — Gran batalla de Cataluña. The Battle of Catalonia (December 1938–February 1939) and the fall of Barcelona.
  • Cap. X — La gran maniobra final del Generalísimo. The final maneuver; the last objectives reached; the Red army destroyed; the end of the war (April 1939).
  • Cap. XI — Resumen y consideraciones generales sobre la victoria nacional. The reasons for Franco’s victory.

Relationship to the Wiki

Aznar gives the wiki the national military history of the Spanish Civil War, written from within the victor’s camp while the fighting had barely ended. Its eleven-plus-twelve-plus-eleven chapters trace the war as a sequence of campaigns and maneuvers — the march on Madrid, Málaga, Guadalajara, the northern front, Brunete, Teruel, the Ebro, Catalonia, the final offensive — narrated from the perspective of the Generalísimo’s staff. Read against Madariaga’s political chronicle of the Republic and Civil War, Chaves Nogales’s literary testimony from the liberal middle, the Azaña prison memoir of 1934, and Tusell’s continuous scholarly history of the dictatorship that followed the victory Aznar celebrates and his companion volume II on the Republic and the war’s politics (revolution, repression, the church, the internationalisation) that Aznar treats from the staff viewpoint, it completes the Spanish cluster with the military-strategic voice of the winning side, complementary in the Spanish section to how the German, Italian and Russian regime sources anchor their national clusters elsewhere in the wiki.
