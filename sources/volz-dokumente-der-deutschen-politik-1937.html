Von der Großmacht zur Weltmacht 1937

Hans Volz (ed.) — Von der Großmacht zur Weltmacht. 1937 (From Great Power to World Power. 1937), Band 5 of Dokumente der deutschen Politik (Documents of German Politics), edited by Paul Meier-Benneckenstein, president of the Hochschule für Politik (Academy for Politics), Berlin. Junker und Dünnhaupt Verlag, Berlin, 1938. An official Nazi compilation of 81 German state documents of the year 1937 — laws, decrees, speeches, notes and protocols — recording the internal consolidation of the National Socialist state and the conduct of German foreign policy in the year before the Anschluss and the Munich crisis.

EditorDr. Hans Volz (1904–1978), head of the library of the Hochschule für Politik, Berlin; series edited by Paul Meier-Benneckenstein, president of the academy; a publication of the Hochschule für Politik / Berlin
SeriesDokumente der deutschen Politik, Band 5 (5th volume of the series, which began in 1935 with the documents of 1933 and thereafter covered each year of the National Socialist regime)
Edition1938, Junker und Dünnhaupt Verlag, Berlin; copyright 1938, printed in Germany; the University of Florida Libraries copy (OCLC 5135115)
Structure81 numbered documents in three main parts with a Zeittafel (chronological table of the documents), an overview of the Führer’s speeches of 1937, and name and subject indexes; 22,426 lines of extracted text
Period1937: documents dated January–December 1937, covering the fourth year of the National Socialist regime and the eve of the great annexations of 1938
NoteA companion to the other Dokumente der deutschen Politik volumes in this wiki — the earlier Friedrichs-compiled Band 1 (Die nationalsozialistische Revolution 1933), Band 2 (Der Aufbau des deutschen Führerstaates 1934), Band 3 (Deutschlands Weg zur Freiheit 1935) and Band 4 (Deutschlands Aufstieg zur Großmacht 1936), and the later Volz Band 6 (Großdeutschland 1938); reproduced from the Hochschule für Politik edition with the original German text of laws, decrees and official communications preserved verbatim

Contents. The volume documents a single year of the Third Reich, 1937, in three parts. Part A (documents 1–11) records the National Socialist state and movement: Heß on the essence of the party, Hitler’s Reichstag speech of 30 January 1937 on the fourth anniversary of the seizure of power, the Reich cabinet memorial session, the award of the Order of the German Eagle, Rosenberg’s congress speech, and the reconstruction of the Reich cabinet of 26 November 1937 (the removal of Blomberg, Fritsch and Neurath and the beginning of Hitler’s personal command of the Wehrmacht). Part B (documents 12–46) records foreign policy: the German replies on the volunteers and control question in the Spanish Civil War, the Non-Intervention Committee speeches of Ribbentrop, relations with Italy and Japan (Mussolini’s visit to Berlin of September 1937, Italy’s adherence to the Anti-Comintern Pact of 6 November 1937), Germany’s relations with her neighbours (Switzerland, Yugoslavia, Bulgaria, Hungary, Poland, the Anglo-German naval agreement, Belgium’s inviolability), and the colonial demand of 1937. Part C (documents 47–81) records the reordering of state, people, economy, culture and law: the Greater Hamburg law and administrative reforms, the German Civil Service law and police law, population and race policy, economic policy, social policy (the Winterhilfswerk, the Ley-Cianetti agreement), labour service and the Wehrmacht, cultural and church policy, and the reform of law, ending with the law of 5 November 1937 on inheritance restrictions for those acting contrary to the community.

Character of the source. An official documentary publication of the National Socialist state, reproducing the original texts of German laws, decrees, speeches, notes and protocols of the year 1937 in the German original, published in Berlin in 1938 by the party-supporting Junker und Dünnhaupt house. It is a primary source in the strictest sense — the laws themselves (the German Civil Service law, the police law, the inheritance-restriction law), the verbatim official communications of the Reich government (the notes on Spain, Belgium’s inviolability, the Anglo-German naval agreement), and the speeches of Hitler, Heß, Göring, Goebbels, Rosenberg, Schacht and others as officially released. Edited from within the regime with the aim of documenting the state’s own account of its activity, it is at once a record of fact and an instrument of propaganda; read alongside the exile histories (such as Rosenberg’s History of the German Republic, also in this wiki), it supplies the official internal voice of the German dictatorship in the interwar period.

Quality. Good. Extracted from a 494-file paginated text dump (University of Florida scan) by concatenating the numbered page files in order. Text is clean and continuous; laws, decrees, official notes and speech texts are preserved verbatim with footnotes. Section and document headings and dates verified against the printed Inhaltsverzeichnis (table of contents).

A. Der nationalsozialistische Staat und die nationalsozialistische Bewegung (The National Socialist State and the National Socialist Movement)

Dokumente 1–11

The internal consolidation of the regime in 1937. Heß on the essence and work of the NSDAP before officers (16 January 1937); Hitler’s Reichstag speech of 30 January 1937 on the fourth anniversary of the National Socialist revolution; the law extending the Enabling Act; the appointment of a chief of the party’s Foreign Organisation; the Reich cabinet memorial session; the reconstruction of the Reich cabinet of 26 November 1937 (Blomberg, Fritsch, Neurath — the foundation of Hitler’s personal command of the Wehrmacht); the Order of the German Eagle; Rosenberg’s congress speech on the struggle between creation and destruction (8 September 1937); Bohle’s speech in London (1 October 1937); Hitler’s speech at the Harvest Festival on the Bückeberg (3 October 1937).

B. Deutschlands Außenpolitik (Germany’s Foreign Policy)

I. Deutschland und die Spanienfrage (Germany and the Spanish Question) — Dokumente 12–26

The German intervention in the Spanish Civil War as officially documented: the reply notes of the Reich government to the French and British demarches on the volunteer question (7 and 25 January 1937); the German notes on the intervention and control system; Ribbentrop’s speeches before the London Non-Intervention Committee (30 July and 16 October 1937); Goebbels’ speech “The Truth about Spain” at the Nuremberg party congress (9 September 1937); the attack on the Deutschland and the German bombardment of Almería; the German decision to leave the control system; the evacuation of volunteers.

II. Deutschlands Verhältnis zu Italien und Japan (Germany’s Relations with Italy and Japan) — Dokumente 27–32

Mussolini’s visit to Berlin and his speech on the Maifeld (28 September 1937) and Hitler’s answering speech; Neurath on the meaning of German-Italian friendship; the German reply to Belgium’s invitation to the Brussels Nine-Power Conference; the protocol of Italy’s adherence to the German-Japanese Anti-Comintern Pact (6 November 1937); the official German declaration on Italy’s withdrawal from the League of Nations (12 December 1937).

III. Deutschland und seine Nachbarn (Germany and her Neighbours) — Dokumente 33–38

Hitler’s declaration to the former Swiss Federal Councillor Schultheß on Germany’s relations with Switzerland (23 February 1937); Neurath’s visits to Belgrade, Sofia and Budapest (June 1937); the German-Polish agreement on Upper Silesia (14 July 1937); the Anglo-German naval agreement of 17 July 1937 and the exchange of information on fleet construction; the German note on Belgium’s inviolability (13 October 1937); the German-Polish minorities agreement of 5 November 1937 and Hitler’s reception of the Polish minority representatives.

IV. Deutschlands Kolonialforderung (Germany’s Colonial Demand) — Dokumente 39–46

The German colonial demand of 1937 as officially stated: Hitler’s Reichstag speech of 30 January 1937, his proclamation at Nuremberg and his speech at the Harvest Festival; Ribbentrop on the Four-Year Plan and world trade; Schacht at the opening of the German House at the Paris World Exhibition; Göring at the opening of the International Chamber of Commerce congress in Berlin; Epp on Germany’s legal claim to her colonial property and on “the significance of space and raw materials.”

C. Die Neuordnung von Staat, Volk, Wirtschaft, Kultur und Recht (The Reordering of State, People, Economy, Culture and Law)

I. Staatsordnung, Verfassung und Verwaltung (State Order, Constitution and Administration) — Dokumente 47–51

The Greater Hamburg law and other territorial adjustments (26 January 1937); Frick’s speech on the law; the Hessian law abolishing the provinces of Starkenburg, Upper Hesse and Rheinhessen; the law on the constitution and administration of the Hanseatic city of Hamburg; Himmler’s broadcast on the tasks of the German police (15 January 1937); the German Civil Service law and the Reich disciplinary code (26 January 1937); the German Police Officer law (24 June 1937); Frick on party and state in the Civil Service law; the law on the Reichsbank and the Reichsbahn (10 February 1937); the law on the term of office of the municipal self-government bodies; the second implementing ordinance of the Reich flag law.

II. Bevölkerungs- und Rassenpolitik (Population and Race Policy) — Dokumente 52–54

Reinhardt on the early marriage of officials at the Reich meeting of the Reich League of Large Families (Frankfurt, 5 June 1937); the law on measures in the former Upper Silesian plebiscite area (30 June 1937); Reinhardt’s article “On the Way to the Reich Family Fund” (December 1937).

III. Wirtschaftspolitik (Economic Policy) — Dokumente 55–62

The law on joint-stock and limited partnerships (the Stock Corporation law) of 30 January 1937; Hitler at the International Automobile and Motorcycle Exhibition; Ribbentrop on the Four-Year Plan and world trade; Göring on measures to increase agricultural production and his speeches at the International Chamber of Commerce congress and before the party’s Foreign Organisation; Göring’s ordinance on the union of mining rights; Funk at the East Fair in Königsberg; Darré on four years of National Socialist agrarian policy; Reinhardt on the German economy under National Socialist leadership; Schacht at the German savings-bank congress in Essen; the table of unemployment at each month-end 1933–1937.

IV. Sozialpolitik (Social Policy) — Dokumente 63–67

The constitution of the Winterhilfswerk (24 March 1937); Goebbels’ report on the Winterhilfswerk 1936/37; Heß on wages and prices (6 June 1937); the Ley-Cianetti agreement (24 June 1937); Ley’s report to the fifth annual meeting of the German Labour Front at Nuremberg and his account of the “Strength through Joy” achievements.

V. Arbeitsdienst und Wehrmacht (Labour Service and the Wehrmacht) — Dokumente 68a–68c

The decree on the Reich Labour Leader (30 January 1937); the law amending the Reich Labour Service law (19 March 1937); the decree on the summer and winter strength of the Reich Labour Service and the labour service for female youth (24 November 1937); the defence tax law (20 July 1937).

VI. Kultur- und Kirchenpolitik (Cultural and Church Policy) — Dokumente 69–78

The decree on the Adolf Hitler Schools and the Ley-Schirach declaration; the decree on a General Building Inspector for the Reich capital; the foundation of a German National Prize for Art and Science and Goebbels’ proclamation of its first holders at the Nuremberg culture congress; the decree convening a constitutive general synod of the German Evangelical Church; Scheel on the tradition and future of the German student body; the German note to the Holy See (29 May 1937); the German protest against the intervention of the Oxford World Church Conference; the decree establishing a College for Politics; Goebbels’ speech at the annual meeting of the Reich Chamber of Culture; Rust at the foundation-laying of the Military-Technical Faculty; Hitler’s speeches at Munich and on the foundation-laying of the faculty; Schirach on German-foreign youth exchange.

VII. Neugestaltung des Rechts (The Reformation of Law) — Dokumente 79–81

Schlegelberger on the reordering of the so-called civil law (Heidelberg, 25 January 1937); the law on inheritance restrictions for those acting contrary to the community (5 November 1937), restricting the compulsory portion of heirs who married Jews or Jewish half-breeds after 1935.

Zeittafel und Chronologie (Chronological Table), Registers

The volume closes with a Zeittafel und Chronologie of the reproduced documents month by month, an overview of the Führer’s speeches in 1937, and Personen- and Sachregister (name and subject indexes), together with a map of the territorial reorganisation of Greater Hamburg and a list of illustrations.
