Rest of Europe 1919–45

A narrative-research knowledge base on continental European history from the end of the First World War and the Paris Peace Conference through the interwar republics, the rise of fascism, and the Second World War. The United Kingdom is treated separately in its own wiki. Pages are grounded in the deposited source corpus — German Weimar and National Socialist documentary and biographical sources, Italian Fascist party histories, Spanish Second Republic and Civil War accounts, French Third Republic analyses and the eyewitness of its collapse, and the Soviet official history of the Stalin era.

Narrative arc

The Great War ended the old order and opened the era the sources of this wiki survey. Weimar Germany was born of the November revolution of 1918 and died in 1930, its republic undone by the burdens of Versailles, the Ruhr crisis and inflation, and the failure of its guardians — the story Rosenberg tells from the left, Heiden from the democratic side. In the wreckage arose the National Socialist movement: Hitler’s Mein Kampf supplied the doctrine of race, the Führerprinzip and Lebensraum; the Beer Hall Putsch of 1923 and the years of reconstruction led to the seizure of power in 1933, the Enabling Act, the Gleichschaltung of state and society, the Nuremberg racial laws of 1935, the remilitarization of the Rhineland of 1936, and the annexations of 1938 — the arc recorded step by step in the regime’s own Dokumente der deutschen Politik.

Italy travelled the parallel road of Fascism: born of interventionism and the Fasci di combattimento of 1919, it passed through the biennio rosso, squadrism, Fiume and the March on Rome of 1922 to the corporate state — the story told from within by Businelli, Volpe, Farinacci and Pini — and ended in the disasters of the Second World War and the coup of 25 July 1943, recorded by Badoglio, and in the testimony of Primo Levi’s Se questo è un uomo.

Spain descended from the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera into the Second Republic (1931), whose reforms and crises — the agrarian and Catalan questions, the October 1934 insurrection, the Popular Front of 1936 — plunged the country into the Civil War, the internationalised conflict of the three Franciscos that ended in Franco’s victory of 1939 and the long dictatorship. Madariaga, Azaña, Chaves Nogales, Fernández Almagro, Aznar and Hayes record it from every side.

France, the old parliamentary Republic of the centre, drifted through the Bloc National and the Cartel des Gauches to the Popular Front and the crisis of 1938 — the “practice of doing nothing” of Daniel Halévy’s chronicle — and collapsed in the strange defeat of 1940, dissected by Bloch, while Élie Halévy diagnosed the era of tyrannies and the Soviet sources told the parallel story of the building of socialism, the purges and the Great Patriotic War.

How to read

  1. Start with major events: the founding of the Fasci (1919), the March on Rome (1922), the Beer Hall Putsch (1923), the seizure of power and Enabling Act (1933), the Nuremberg laws (1935), the Anschluss and Munich (1938), the Spanish Civil War campaigns (1936–39), the coup of 25 July 1943, the Great Patriotic War (1941–45).
  2. Read entities as continuous political biographies tied to those crises.
  3. Use concepts for structures that span decades: national-socialism, fascism-italian, the totalitarian-state and era-of-tyrannies, the second-spanish-republic, the third-republic-france, weimar-republic.
  4. Every entity/event/concept page ends with a Sources section pointing at the underlying books.
  5. Native-language glosses follow on first use of each technical term.
